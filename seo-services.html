What this service is for

The work should answer a business question, not fill a checklist.

SEO is not one task and it is not a ranking guarantee. I help businesses understand how their important pages perform in search, identify the highest-priority opportunities and improve the technical, content and internal-linking foundations that support visibility over time.

I begin with the business goal and the search results, not with a fixed checklist. A realistic SEO plan may start with a technical fix, a better service page, a keyword map or a measurement issue. The scope is agreed before implementation.

Common problems this service can address

  • A website has several pages targeting the same broad phrase, so relevance is diluted.
  • Important service pages are thin, difficult to navigate or written without search intent in mind.
  • Technical warnings are mixed with low-priority suggestions and nobody knows what to fix first.
  • International ambitions are being handled with duplicate country pages rather than useful market context.

In practice

How I prioritise SEO work

SEO recommendations are ordered by the relationship between technical access, search intent and business value. A page that cannot be crawled or understood needs a different fix from a page that is visible but attracts the wrong searcher. A service page with the right audience but no next step needs a different improvement from a blog article that answers a question but never supports the commercial journey.

I also look at what the search results already reward. Search volume alone cannot tell us whether a query is useful for your business. The page format, competing domains, local or video features, questions on the SERP and the strength of the current site all shape a realistic opportunity. The result is a plan that connects a query to a page, a page to an internal link and a page to a measurable action.

A good SEO handover should help you make decisions after the project ends. It should distinguish a critical indexability issue from a nice-to-have suggestion, explain why a page is being changed and show which work can wait. That is how I keep the service useful for a small team with limited time.

Inputs and review

What I need and how we review it

For SEO work, useful inputs include access to the site or a representative set of URLs, the services or products that matter commercially, priority markets, known competitors and any existing Search Console or Analytics context. If access is not available, I can still provide a research-led recommendation, but implementation findings will be more limited.

Review is based on the agreed pages and actions: indexability and technical findings, search-intent fit, content and metadata improvements, internal-link coverage and the measurement questions left for the next phase.

Questions clients usually ask

Before you decide, here is the useful detail.

How long does SEO take to show results?
There is no honest universal timeline. Technical corrections can be completed quickly, while search visibility and qualified enquiries normally require a consistent period of useful content, sound architecture and measurement. I will separate quick fixes from longer-term work.
Can you provide international SEO services?
Yes. International projects can include market-aware keyword research, page architecture, language and location considerations, hreflang recommendations when genuinely needed, and content that serves more than one market without duplication.
Do you build backlinks?
I do not sell bulk or artificial link packages. If authority work is relevant, the plan focuses on useful content, legitimate relationships, relevant citations and a realistic outreach approach.
Can you optimise an existing website?
Yes. An existing website can be audited and improved without a full rebuild when its structure is workable. I will identify which pages should be kept, rewritten, consolidated or replaced.
