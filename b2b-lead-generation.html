What this service is for

The work should answer a business question, not fill a checklist.

A long spreadsheet is not automatically a useful lead list. I help define who is worth pursuing, research relevant companies and contacts, organise the information and apply a practical verification process so a sales team can review the work before outreach begins.

The most important part of lead generation happens before research: agreeing what a qualified prospect means. I build the criteria with you, test a small sample, refine the rules and only then scale the research. That makes the final delivery easier to inspect and improve.

Common problems this service can address

  • Lead lists are judged by row count rather than fit with the actual offer.
  • Contacts have no role context, source, verification status or reason they might care.
  • Research is repeated manually by sales people with inconsistent criteria.
  • Outreach starts without considering consent, privacy, platform rules or regional requirements.

In practice

Why relevance matters more than a large list

B2B research becomes useful when the prospect can be connected to the problem your offer solves. Company size, industry and location may be necessary filters, but they are not enough by themselves. The decision-maker role, current business context, trigger, technology or service model may explain why an account belongs in the list.

I prefer to test the criteria on a small sample before scaling. That catches ambiguous terms, irrelevant directories, duplicate companies and roles that look senior but do not influence the purchase. It also creates a chance for your sales team to say what makes a record useful before the final batch is delivered.

The final file should be easy to review. Source fields, segmentation, missing information and verification status make the list more honest than a sheet that presents every row as equally certain. Better research can improve the starting point for outreach, but the message, consent, timing and sales process still belong to the business.

Inputs and review

What I need and how we review it

For prospect research, I need the offer, ICP, target markets, company size or segment rules, relevant roles, exclusions, fields required and the way the list will be used. A sample of existing good or bad prospects is often more useful than a vague industry label.

Review is based on fit, relevance, source context, duplicate rate, field completeness, verification status and whether the delivery can be reviewed by the sales team. A row count is never the only measure.
