A keyword plan that prevents content from competing with itself

The purpose of this project was to replace topic guesswork with a usable content and SEO roadmap. Rather than handing over an unfiltered list of phrases, the work mapped valuable searches to the one page most able to satisfy them. That gives a client a clearer order of operations: which existing pages need attention, which new pages have a defensible role and which ideas should wait.

Research began with the customer’s language and the search results

Seed topics were expanded across services, customer problems and comparison questions. Each term was classified by informational, commercial or transactional intent, then considered alongside difficulty and funnel stage. Long-tail opportunities received particular attention because a newer or less authoritative domain often benefits from precise, useful topics before it tries to compete for broad head terms.

SERP observation added context that a keyword volume figure cannot supply. The visible formats, competing page types, recurring subtopics and apparent search purpose informed whether a term belonged in a service page, guide, comparison, FAQ or a supporting section. This prevents a content calendar from being based solely on numbers detached from what people actually see when they search.

One primary home for each meaningful topic

The mapping process checked for cannibalization and assigned each priority term to exactly one primary URL. Existing pages could be strengthened where their purpose already matched; new content was reserved for genuine gaps. A six-month calendar then turned that map into an achievable sequence, with an internal-linking plan connecting deeper educational material back to the appropriate commercial or service page.

The documented outcome is a filterable keyword-to-page map with intent, difficulty and priority noted for the client’s language and market. It is a planning asset, not a promise that every target will rank. Publishing quality, technical condition, competition and the site’s existing authority still affect search performance.
