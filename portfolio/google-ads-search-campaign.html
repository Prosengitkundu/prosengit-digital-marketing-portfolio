Project questions

Can the same approach be used in another industry?

The planning principles can transfer, but the research, language, audience and acceptance criteria must be adapted. Reusing a process is sensible; copying assumptions, creative or keywords without market evidence is not.

Does this case study guarantee the same outcome?

No. It documents the existing project scope and delivered status. Search, advertising, sales and website outcomes depend on factors beyond a single deliverable. Any new project should begin with its own baseline and written scope.

A search campaign blueprint built around real buying intent

This project planned a Google Search Ads account before spend was scaled. The objective was to capture people actively looking for a service while giving the client a disciplined way to learn which searches deserve budget. The work is a production-ready blueprint, not a report of live campaign returns: its value is in the account decisions made before ads begin competing in an auction.

Search terms were organized by intent, not by a loose keyword list

Keyword research was mapped into tight ad groups so that the query, ad message and landing-page promise could stay closely aligned. The initial match-type approach favored phrase match with clear expansion rules, providing control while data is still limited. A negative keyword list was just as important: employment searches, research-only intent, irrelevant locations and other poor-fit queries were identified as exclusions to reduce avoidable waste.

Responsive search ad variants were written around the language of the searcher and the offer’s strongest proof points. Where it made sense, keyword insertion could reinforce relevance without producing awkward copy. Sitelinks, callouts and structured snippets were planned to answer common questions directly in the results page and to create more useful paths to high-intent pages.

Measurement was treated as part of the campaign, not an afterthought

The plan connected conversion tracking and lead-value assignment to the decision of what counts as a meaningful response. Budget pacing and a recurring optimization schedule set expectations for what should be reviewed: search terms, negative keywords, ad combinations, spend distribution and conversion quality. That routine keeps the account from being treated as a “set and forget” channel.

The outcome recorded here is a structured campaign plan with keyword-to-ad-group mapping, negative controls, ad assets, tracking requirements and a budget-review cadence. Actual cost per lead and return depend on factors outside the blueprint, including the offer, landing page, market, bids and sales follow-up.
